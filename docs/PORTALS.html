Where everything lives.

Four surfaces, one API. Staging nests under a single dev label rather than sitting beside production, and that choice is about cookies, not tidiness.

Why dev nests

Name staging app-dev.anystudio.ai and every host is a sibling under anystudio.ai — so any cookie ever set on the parent domain is readable by both environments. One careless domain=.anystudio.ai and a staging bug can read production sessions.

Under *.dev.anystudio.ai the two trees cannot see each other's cookies, and one wildcard certificate still covers the whole staging estate.

Admin is isolated on purpose

The admin portal gets its own cookie, its own session store and its own login. A stolen app.anystudio.ai session must be worth nothing at admin.anystudio.ai. No shared parent-domain cookie, ever — that single decision is what keeps a customer-side compromise from becoming a company-side one.

02

User portal

app.anystudio.ai

Written for Bimbo, who sells fabric in Lagos, works from her phone between customers, and has never opened a design tool. Everything below has to make sense to her before it makes sense to us.

Her first fifteen minutes

  1. She arrives already signed inStraight from sign-up. A workspace was created for her — type BUSINESS because she said she sells products — so there is never a personal account that later has to "become" a business.
  2. Three questions, all skippableBusiness name, what she sells, and the currency she prices in. Skipping leaves defaults she can fix later; blocking her here loses her.
  3. The empty state is a taskNot an illustration. One button: add your first product photo. That is the only thing to do on this screen.
  4. She uploads the photo she already hasTaken on a table, under a ceiling light. She adds a name and a price. Category and tone are optional.
  5. She picks what she wants backBranded images, background change, description, caption, reel — each with its credit cost beside it, and a running total. She sees the price before she spends, not after.
  6. The job runs where she can watch itQueued → generating → finishing, with real states rather than a spinner. She can leave; it finishes without her and notifies.
  7. The sheet comes backEvery output on one screen. Per item: download, tweak, make a variation, or publish. Her free allowance ticks down visibly — two of three left.

What she can reach from the sidebar

TodayBalance, recent work, and anything that needs her: a failed job, an Instagram connection about to expire, a low balance.
CreateThe generation flow. Pick an existing product or start from a new photo.
LibraryEvery asset ever produced, filterable by product, type and date. Re-edit, re-export at another size, bulk download.
ProductsHer catalogue. A product holds its photo, name, price and category, and every generation attaches to it — so a price change does not mean re-typing.
Brand kitLogo, colours, fonts, tone, watermark. Applied to everything automatically, with a live sample so she can see the effect before she commits.
PublishingConnected accounts, scheduled posts, and what actually went out. Instagram, TikTok and WhatsApp Status.
InsightsGenerations over time, credits by output type, and — once publishing data returns — which posts did anything.
Credits & billingBalance, the full ledger, top-ups, plan changes and invoices, all in her own currency.
SettingsProfile, phone and email, workspace, teammates, notifications, and the destructive things kept well away from everything else.

The four flows that have to be right

Spending a credit

Cost shown before she commits. Debit on success. On failure the credit returns and the interface says so in words — "That one failed, your 10 credits are back" — because silence here reads as theft.

Running out mid-way

Her input is never lost. The generation is saved as a draft, she tops up, and she comes back to exactly the screen she left, with her selections intact.

Publishing

Connect once, then preview per platform before anything posts. Nothing is ever published without a confirmation step. Expired tokens surface on Today, not at the moment she tries to post.

WhatsApp parity

Everything she makes in WhatsApp appears here, in the same library, because her phone number is the account. The web is a bigger screen for the same workspace — never a second, separate life.

The ledger is the product

Every row she sees — date, what it was, plus or minus, balance after — comes from ledger_entries, which is append-only. There is no balance column to disagree with the history.

When she disputes a charge, the ledger is the answer. That is only true if we never wrote a shortcut.

03

Organization portal

org.anystudio.ai

Two people share this screen: the platform PM who signed, and the engineer who has to make it work. The engineer decides whether it stays.

The engineer's first hour

  1. Lands with an organization already createdMade during sign-up, workspace type ORGANIZATION, with the region and billing currency already chosen.
  2. Creates a projectOne project per environment — marketplace-staging, marketplace-prod — each with its own keys, quota, rate limit and webhook URL.
  3. Takes the test keySecret shown exactly once, with a copy button and a curl that works unedited. 500 test credits, no card.
  4. Makes the first call and watches it landThe request appears in the request log within seconds: endpoint, latency, credit cost, request id. That feedback loop is what earns the integration.
  5. Points a webhook at their own systemSigned payload, delivery log, replay button. Generation completion is pushed, never polled.
  6. Runs a real batch against stagingA few hundred of their own listings. Per-merchant usage shows up immediately, so the PM can check the billing model before anyone signs.
  7. Requests live keysThe only step that asks for documents: registration number and billing entity. Everything before this was free.

What the portal holds

OverviewUsage against commitment, spend, error rate, p50 and p95 latency, and anything currently degraded.
ProjectsEnvironment, quota, rate limit, webhook, usage. Deleting one is deliberately hard.
API keysKey id, created, last used, scopes, per-key rate limit, revoke. Secrets are hashed with Argon2id and shown once.
MerchantsThe sub-accounts. Generations, credits and cost per merchant — what makes per-merchant billing real rather than promised.
RequestsRecent calls with status, latency, key, merchant and request id. Filterable, exportable. The single biggest reason support tickets never get written.
WebhooksEndpoint, signing secret, delivery history with attempts, and replay for anything that failed while their server was down.
Usage & billingMetered by endpoint, project and merchant. Commitment versus overage, invoices, CSV export for their finance team.
Brand kitsOrganization defaults with per-merchant overrides, so a marketplace can hold a house style and still let a seller keep their own.
TeamMembers, the five roles, invitations, and joining by verified email domain.
Audit logKey created or revoked, member added, role changed, project deleted — who, when, from where.
SettingsOrganization profile, region, billing entity, data retention, and SSO when it arrives.

Roles

Key rotation overlaps, never cuts over

Creating a new key leaves the old one live. Both work until the customer revokes the old one themselves. A hard cutover means somebody's storefront stops generating at 2am, and they will remember that longer than they remember the feature.

Quotas warn before they bite

A soft limit emails and shows a banner. The hard limit returns 429 with RateLimit-Reset and Retry-After, both documented. Nobody should discover a quota by watching their queue back up.

04

Admin portal

admin.anystudio.ai

Internal only. One principle runs through all of it: staff can read nearly everything and change nearly nothing without giving a reason that gets written down.

What support actually does, in order

  1. A customer says a generation looks wrongSupport searches by phone number, email or request id.
  2. Opens the jobInput, provider, model, credit cost, timings, error, output — the whole record, without touching the customer's account.
  3. Reproduces itRe-runs the job against the same provider, or against a different one, on the company's credits rather than the customer's.
  4. Fixes the moneyA refund is an ADJUSTMENT row with a reason string. Nothing is edited. Above a threshold it needs a second person.
  5. Everything is written downEvery step above leaves an audit row: who, what, when, from which address, and why.

Areas

OperationsQueue depth, jobs running and failed, provider health and latency, and today's provider spend against revenue.
People & workspacesSearch, plan, balance, flags. Read-only impersonation that is time-boxed, logged, and visible in the customer's own security log.
LedgersAny wallet's full history. Adjustments require a reason and append a row — the ledger is never edited.
GenerationsInspect and re-run any job. The only honest way to answer "why did it do that".
ProvidersEnable, disable, reroute models, set cost per model, watch circuit breakers. Config rows, so a provider outage is a toggle rather than a deploy.
Plans & pricingPlan definitions, price per market, credit cost per operation — versioned and effective-dated so old invoices still reconcile.
PaymentsTransactions, failures, refunds and disputes across Flutterwave and Paddle.
OrganizationsCommitment, usage, overage, contract state. Issue credits, adjust rate limits.
AbuseFlagged generations, repeat offenders, blocked numbers. Free-tier farming lands here first.
Feature flagsPer workspace and per organization, so a risky change reaches ten accounts before ten thousand.
Audit logImmutable, searchable, exportable. The one thing in this portal with no delete.
SystemMigration state, queue lag, storage use, connection pools.

Staff roles

RoleAdds
SupportRead everything; resend verification; read-only impersonation
OperatorRe-run jobs, refund up to a capped amount, resolve abuse flags
AdminProvider routing, plans and pricing, organization limits
SuperadminManage staff accounts and roles. Two people minimum, never one
Non-negotiable

Mandatory TOTP on every staff account. Two-hour idle sessions. Step-up re-authentication before any money movement or role change. A reason string on every mutation.

Impersonation is read-only by default and always visible to the customer afterwards. If staff need to act as someone, that is a separate, louder permission — not a checkbox on the same screen.

05

Logging you can actually debug from.

Pino, JSON, one line per event. The goal is that a customer quotes a code from an error screen and support finds the exact request in one search.

  • Every request gets a ULID returned in x-request-id and shown in the UI on any error. It propagates into the queued job, so one id covers the whole chain from tap to generated file.
  • Fixed context on every line — request id, workspace, actor (user id, or api key plus merchant), route, status, duration, credit cost.
  • Warn means recovered but notable — a provider fell back, a retry succeeded, a soft quota was crossed, a webhook needed a second attempt. If nobody would act on it, it is info.
  • Errors are logged once, at the boundary, with the stack. Log-and-rethrow at every layer turns one failure into five and buries the cause.
  • Sinks — stdout to the host, shipped to Better Stack or Axiom. Not Datadog at this size; the bill outgrows the value.

Redaction

Two layers, because a named list alone always misses something eventually.

// packages/shared/src/logging/redact.ts
// Layer 1 — exact paths pino strips before serialising.
export const REDACT_PATHS = [
  'req.headers.authorization', 'req.headers.cookie', 'res.headers["set-cookie"]',
  'req.headers["x-api-key"]', '*.password', '*.secret', '*.token',
  '*.refreshToken', '*.otp', '*.cardNumber', '*.signedUrl',
  'req.body.imageBase64',
]

// Layer 2 — a deep scrub for anything the list did not predict.
// Keys matching this are replaced wherever they appear, at any depth.
const SENSITIVE = /secret|token|password|passwd|api[-_]?key|authorization|otp|cvv|pan/i

// Identifiers are kept useful rather than deleted outright:
// phone  +2348012345678  →  +234****5678   (still matchable in support)
// email  ada@shop.ng     →  a**@shop.ng    (domain survives for debugging)
Two that are easy to miss

A signed R2 URL is a credential. Logging one hands anyone with log access the file. Log the object key, never the signed link.

Prompts must be logged — they are how a bad generation gets diagnosed — but they carry whatever the customer typed, which includes phone numbers and addresses. The scrub runs over prompt text too.

Rate limiting, per surface.

Redis token buckets with a burst of twice the steady rate, because merchants batch. One shared limit across all four surfaces would be either useless or hostile.

SurfaceKeyed onLimitOn exceed
Sign-inIP + account5 / min, 10 / hr429, then lockout with email
OTP sendPhone number3 / hr429, cooldown shown in UI
App readsUser120 / min429, retry transparently
Generation submitWorkspace3 concurrentQueued, position shown
Org APIAPI keyBy plan tier429 + rate-limit headers
Org APIand merchant idPer-merchant ceiling429 for that merchant only
Inbound webhooksNot limited—Signature + idempotent by event id
AdminStaff account300 / min429 and a security alert
The second org limit is the important one

Limiting only by API key lets one runaway merchant on a marketplace consume the whole platform's quota and starve every other seller on it. The per-merchant ceiling means a bad integration hurts one shop, not the marketplace's entire catalogue.

An admin hitting a limit is a signal

Staff do not legitimately make three hundred requests a minute. That 429 raises an alert rather than just refusing the call, because the likeliest explanation is a compromised account.

07

Security

The header policy already exists in design/seo/next-headers.mjs. These are the decisions that live in application code.

CORS, per surface

  • App API — an explicit allowlist of app, org and admin origins for the matching environment. No wildcards, credentials enabled.
  • Public org API — no CORS headers at all. This is deliberate. Refusing browser calls means a merchant physically cannot ship their secret key in frontend JavaScript, which is the most common way API keys leak.
  • Marketing — no credentialed endpoints, so nothing to allow.

Clickjacking and framing

  • frame-ancestors 'none' plus X-Frame-Options: DENY on all four surfaces.
  • Nonce-based CSP per surface; the admin policy is the strictest and allows no third-party scripts whatsoever.

Sessions and secrets

  • Cookies are __Host- prefixed, HttpOnly, Secure, SameSite=Lax, host-scoped with no leading dot.
  • CSRF: double-submit token on every state-changing route for cookie-authenticated surfaces. The org API is bearer-authenticated, so CSRF does not apply there.
  • API key secrets are Argon2id hashes. Provider keys live in the host's secret manager and are never present in the web app's environment.
The hole I would have shipped

Organization onboarding asks the buyer to paste a product URL from their storefront, and the server fetches it. Unguarded, that is server-side request forgery — a link to 169.254.169.254 or an internal address, fetched by our own infrastructure with our own network position.

Every outbound fetch of a customer-supplied URL goes through one guarded client: public IPs only, no redirects to private ranges, DNS re-resolved after redirect, hard timeout, size cap. One helper, used everywhere, never fetch() directly.

Uploads

Verify magic bytes rather than trusting the content type, cap dimensions and file size, and strip EXIF — phone photos carry GPS coordinates, and a seller posting a product should not be publishing their home address.

08

Pipeline, containers, migrations.

Images

Multi-stage, non-root, one Dockerfile each for api and worker. The web app goes to Vercel and needs no image.

# apps/api/Dockerfile — deps → build → runtime
FROM node:22-slim AS deps
# pnpm fetch first, so a lockfile-only change reuses the layer

FROM deps AS build
# prisma generate MUST run before tsc, or typecheck fails on missing client
RUN pnpm db:generate && pnpm --filter @anystudio/api build

FROM node:22-slim AS runtime
USER node
HEALTHCHECK CMD node -e "fetch('http://localhost:3001/health')"
CMD ["node", "dist/main.js"]

Migrations

Never in the start command

Two replicas booting together both run prisma migrate deploy, and concurrent migrations against one database is how a schema ends up half-applied.

Migrations run as a separate one-shot release job that must succeed before new containers roll. The application's start command only starts the application.

Seeding

Idempotent, upsert-only, safe to run on every deploy. Split by what it is for:

  • Reference data, every environment — plans, prices per market, credit costs per operation, provider and model rows. These are configuration the application reads, so they belong in the database rather than in constants.
  • Fixtures, guarded by SEED_ENV=dev — a demo workspace, a seeded wallet, sample products, one organization with a test key. Production refuses to run this branch.

CI

# .github/workflows/ci.yml
install → db:generate → lint → typecheck → test
       → build (turbo, affected only)
       → docker build + push  [main only]
       → migrate (one-shot job)
       → deploy api + worker   [dev auto · prod on approval]

turbo-ignore on Vercel so the marketing site does not rebuild when only the worker changed.

Local

One docker-compose.yml with Postgres, Redis, MinIO standing in for R2, and Mailpit for mail. pnpm dev brings up the API, the worker and all four Next.js apps on separate ports, seeded and ready.

09

What I build first.

In this order, because each one is hard to change later and cheap to get right now.

  1. Monorepo skeleton and DockerTurborepo, pnpm workspaces, the two Dockerfiles, docker-compose, and CI that goes green on an empty app.
  2. Prisma schema and the ledgerEvery table from the data model, plus the Postgres functions that lock the wallet row and append debits and refunds in one transaction. Tested against concurrent spends before anything calls them.
  3. Migrations and seedsThe release job, reference data, and the dev fixtures.
  4. The API skeletonRequest ids, the logger with redaction, the rate-limit guards, CORS per surface, the health endpoint — the cross-cutting layer, before a single feature route.
  5. AuthPhone and email, sessions per surface, and the admin's separate stricter path.

Every route gets a comment block above it saying what it does, who may call it, what it costs in credits, and what it writes to the ledger. Not a description of the code — a description of the consequence.
